Toddler not responding to their name: what it can mean

Checked by an HCPC-registered occupational therapist · October 2026

Most babies turn when they hear their name by around their first birthday, but plenty of toddlers ignore it when they're absorbed in play. The first step is always to check their hearing. If hearing is fine and they rarely respond, even when they're not busy, it's one of the signs professionals look at, especially alongside others.

Common reasons a toddler doesn't respond

  • Deep focus: young children can be so absorbed in a game that they really don't register their name.
  • Hearing: glue ear, a build-up of fluid behind the eardrum, is very common in young children and can come and go.
  • Too much noise: a busy room makes it harder for any child to pick out their name.
  • Differences in social attention: some children are less tuned in to voices and faces. This is the reason professionals take it seriously when it happens often.

A simple check at home

  1. Choose a quiet moment when they're playing but not completely absorbed.
  2. Stand behind or to the side of them and say their name once, in a normal voice.
  3. Wait five seconds. Do they turn, look up, or pause?
  4. Try again later in the day, then on a few different days.

Note whether they respond to other sounds, such as a favourite TV theme or a crisp packet opening. If they turn for those but not their name, mention it, because it suggests their hearing is working.

When to talk to someone

  • They rarely respond to their name by around 12 to 18 months, even in quiet moments
  • They don't respond to other everyday sounds either. Ask for a hearing test.
  • It comes with other signs, such as little pointing, few words, or not looking where you point
  • They used to respond and have stopped

Speak to your health visitor or GP, and ask for a hearing test first. A not-responding-to-name sign only counts towards an autism assessment once hearing has been checked as normal.

How to support your child

  • Get down to their level and gently into their line of sight before you speak.
  • Say their name, then something interesting straight away: "Teddy, bubbles!"
  • Play name games, such as peekaboo or songs with their name in them.
  • Cut background noise, such as the TV, when you want their attention.
