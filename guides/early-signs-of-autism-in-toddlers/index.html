Early signs of autism in toddlers: what parents notice

Checked by an HCPC-registered occupational therapist · October 2026

Early signs of autism usually show up in three areas: how a child communicates, how they play and interact, and how they react to routines and their senses. Many toddlers show one or two of these on their own as part of ordinary development. What professionals look for is a pattern of several signs together, over time.

Communicating

  • Not responding to their name, even though their hearing is fine
  • Late babbling or talking, for example fewer than ten words by age 2
  • Losing words they used to say
  • Repeating words or phrases they've heard (called echolalia) rather than using them to ask for things
  • Using few gestures, such as pointing, waving or nodding

Playing and being with others

  • Not often bringing or pointing at things to share them with you
  • Not looking where you point, or looking back at you to share a moment
  • Less eye contact or smiling back than you'd expect
  • Playing alone, with little interest in other children
  • Little pretend play, such as feeding a teddy or talking on a toy phone
  • Not copying what others do

Routines, movement and senses

  • Repetitive movements, such as hand flapping, rocking, spinning or finger flicking
  • Playing the same way again and again, such as lining up toys or opening and closing doors
  • Very strong reactions to small changes, and needing things to stay the same
  • Strong reactions to sounds, textures, smells or clothes, or seeming not to notice pain or noise
  • Very strong reactions to the taste, texture or look of food

Remember

Every child develops at their own pace, and none of these signs means a child is autistic on its own. Girls often show fewer obvious signs, and some children only stand out once they start nursery or school.

What to do if you've noticed several of these

  1. Write down what you see: when it happens, how often, and what happened just before. A short video on your phone helps even more.
  2. Talk to your health visitor or GP. Ask for a hearing test first if your child doesn't respond to their name.
  3. Ask nursery whether they see the same things. Their view often helps a referral.
  4. Follow the NHS route to an assessment. Our free roadmap shows each step and the usual waiting times.
