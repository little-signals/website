How to get an autism assessment for your child on the NHS

Checked by an HCPC-registered occupational therapist · October 2026

In England, the usual route starts with your health visitor or GP, goes through a community paediatrician, and ends with an assessment by a specialist team. From a parent's first worry to an answer often takes 1½ to 3 years or more, so it helps to know the steps and to get support in place while you wait.

The usual steps

  1. Raise your concern. For under-5s, your health visitor is often the quickest first step. Nursery staff can raise concerns too, and in some areas they or the health visitor can refer your child directly.
  2. See your GP. A short appointment where the GP listens, may check things like hearing, and usually refers your child to community paediatrics, sometimes called the Child Development Centre.
  3. Wait for the community paediatrician. Often months, sometimes over a year. You should get a letter confirming the referral; chase it if nothing arrives within 6 weeks.
  4. First paediatrician appointment. They look at your child's general development and health, and may refer for a hearing test, speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, or an autism assessment.
  5. Wait for the autism assessment. Usually the longest wait. NICE guidance says it should start within 3 months of referral, but in March 2026 about 90% of open autism referrals in England had waited longer than 13 weeks.
  6. The assessment. Usually more than one appointment, with a team that may include a paediatrician, a speech and language therapist, a psychologist and sometimes an occupational therapist. It typically includes a long parent interview, reports from nursery or school, and a structured play-based observation.
  7. The outcome. The team will tell you whether your child is autistic, isn't, or that they'd like to review again in 6 to 12 months. You get a written report and a feedback appointment.

Things many parents don't know

  • Support doesn't need a diagnosis. Nursery and school SENCOs can put SEN support in place based on need alone, and you can ask the council directly for an EHC needs assessment, which has a legal 20-week timescale.
  • Right to Choose (England only). You can ask your GP to refer your child to a different NHS-funded provider for the assessment, which can be quicker. Those providers often have their own waiting lists too.
  • Disability Living Allowance (DLA) depends on the care your child needs, not on a diagnosis, so families can apply before one.

What helps along the way

  • Keep a diary of what you notice, with dates. Short videos are even better.
  • Write down your referral date and reference, and chase if you hear nothing.
  • Ask nursery for their observations in writing. Assessment teams value them.
  • Bring your notes and questions to every appointment.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have similar steps, but the details and waiting times differ.
