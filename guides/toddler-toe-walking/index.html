Toddler walking on tiptoes: what it can mean

Checked by an HCPC-registered occupational therapist · October 2026

Walking on tiptoes is common in young children, especially between 10 and 18 months when they're learning to walk, and it's usually a habit rather than a problem. Many children grow out of it by around 6 or 7. The key check is whether your child can put their heels flat on the floor when standing.

Why children walk on tiptoes

  • Learning to balance: many new walkers go up on their toes while they find their feet.
  • Habit: often there's no clear reason, and it can run in families.
  • Sensory reasons: some children dislike the feel of certain floors, or enjoy the feeling of being on their toes.
  • Tight calf muscles: less often, the muscles or tendons are tight, which makes heels-down harder.
  • In a small number of children, it's linked to a neurological or developmental difference.

A simple check at home

  1. Ask your child to stand still, barefoot, on a hard floor.
  2. See whether both heels can rest flat on the floor without strain.
  3. Watch them walk a few steps when they're relaxed. Do their heels ever touch down?

When to talk to someone

  • Your child can't put their heels flat on the floor when standing
  • It's only on one side, or they seem stiff, clumsy or in pain
  • They walked normally before and have started walking on tiptoes
  • It comes with other things you've noticed, such as late talking, strong reactions to textures or noise, or not responding to their name

Your GP can check their legs and may refer them to a children's physiotherapist or podiatrist. If you've also noticed differences in communication or play, mention those too, because professionals look at the whole picture.

How to support your child

  • Barefoot play on different surfaces, such as grass, sand and carpet, helps feet get used to new feelings.
  • Heel games: walk like a penguin or a giant on your heels together.
  • Gentle calf stretches can help stop the muscles tightening. Ask a physiotherapist to show you how.
  • Film a short clip of them walking barefoot so you can show your GP what you see.
